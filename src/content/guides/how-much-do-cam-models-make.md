Search this question and you find two kinds of answer: success stories with six-figure
months, and "it depends". Both are useless if you are actually deciding whether to try
camming. This guide sticks to what can be checked: **what sites pay per token**, and **how
many people are really watching** — measured by us, not estimated.

## What the site pays you

Free cam sites run on tokens. Viewers buy them, spend them in your room, and the site
credits you a fixed amount per token.

On Chaturbate — the largest site we track — the rate is **$0.05 per token**, and the site
pays out once you have earned at least **$50**, which is 1,000 tokens. Viewers pay more
than that for each token (two price listings agree on $10.99 for 100 and $79.99 for 1,000),
and the difference is the site's cut, put at roughly 40–50% of what viewers spend.

So the basic conversion is simple:

| Tokens received | You earn (at $0.05) |
|---|---|
| 100 | $5 |
| 1,000 | $50 — the payout minimum |
| 10,000 | $500 |
| 100,000 | $5,000 |

Other sites use their own tokens and rates. Most don't publish them openly, so we don't
quote numbers we can't check.

## How many people are watching

Income depends on how many viewers show up and how many of them tip. Sites don't publish
either number, but they do show how many people are in each room. On 18 November 2026 at
02:00 UTC we recorded every live Chaturbate room:

| Viewers in the room | Share of live rooms (7,046 that report viewers) |
|---|---|
| Fewer than 10 | 51% |
| 10–49 | 40% |
| 50–99 | 4% |
| 100–999 | 4% |
| 1,000 or more | 0.5% |

The top 1% of rooms held **72% of everyone watching**, and the top 10% held 87%. That is
the key fact about camming income: it is not spread out. A small group of performers earns
most of the money, and a typical room has an audience you could count on two hands.

On a smaller site the picture can look different — on CamSoda only 2% of rooms had fewer
than 10 viewers in the same check — but there are also far fewer rooms (1,433 against
Chaturbate's 7,209), and sites don't necessarily count viewers the same way.

## What actually moves the number

From watching thousands of rooms, the patterns are consistent:

- **Hours online.** Rooms that are live often and on a regular schedule build a following;
  our model pages show each performer's usual hours for exactly this reason.
- **Being findable.** Tags, a clear room title and a category with less competition matter.
  Big general categories are crowded; a [niche](/cams/) is not.
- **The room, not just the show.** Goals, a tip menu and an interactive toy give viewers
  a reason to tip in small amounts, which is how most token income arrives.
- **More than one site.** Many performers broadcast on two sites to reach both audiences.
